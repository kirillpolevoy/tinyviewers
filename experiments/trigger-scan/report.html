Jev vs Claude: trigger scenes in Finding Nemo

What the runs show

  • The first Jev setup was mis-designed, and part of the first scoring was unfair to it. The reference list touches 39 of the 60 windows and Jev flagged 39; comparing whole 1–2 minute windows with tightly bounded reference scenes made correct flags look like wasted minutes. The questions also asked about topics and standing states ("is a child taken from a parent") where Jev needs a narrow present-tense event. Details in AUDIT.md.
  • Redesigned Jev (jev-v2-beats) finds the most scenes. Judging short beats of at most 8 subtitle lines with 21 narrow yes/no questions, it found 96% of reference scenes and 94% of the serious ones, flagged 33 minutes (the reference list covers 37), and cost $0.014 in 3 seconds. Sonnet on the whole transcript found 78%, flagged 29 minutes, and cost $0.22 in about 2 minutes.
  • Sonnet is still the most precise. 90% of the time it flags is inside a reference scene (Jev v2: 69%), it flagged none of the 6 calm controls (Jev v2: 2), and 86% of its category labels were right (Jev v2: 48%).
  • Jev's category labels are its weak point. Narrow literal questions still fire on innocent matches: "Dad, you can go now" reads as a companion being told to leave. That needs per-question thresholds tuned on other films.
  • Jev gives the same answer twice; Sonnet does not. Two identical Jev runs agreed on 94% of flagged time, two Sonnet runs on 70%.
  • What did not matter for Jev: removing the character glossary, the previous-window lines, or the cue ids and timestamps from the request changed results by noise only. The sound captions of the hearing-impaired track barely mattered either, and no caption in this film indicates a flash, so flashing lights stayed unassessable.

Treat the numbers as provisional. The reference list was drafted by Claude and has not had a human pass, which favours the Claude analyzers. The Jev redesign was developed while looking at this film's results, which favours Jev. Finding Nemo is also a film Sonnet almost certainly knows well; Jev only sees the lines it is given. One film is an anecdote.
